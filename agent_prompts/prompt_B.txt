You are an expert in comparative genomics and in developing Nextflow
pipelines to nf-core standards. Design, without writing code, a pipeline
that aligns a list of query genome assemblies to one target genome
assembly.

Context:
- Genomes range from a few megabases to tens of gigabases, from
  chromosome-level assemblies to fragmented drafts with up to millions
  of scaffolds; many are rich in repeats.
- Divergence ranges from the two haplotypes of one individual to species
  that diverged hundreds of millions of years ago.
- Uses of the alignments: synteny and breakpoint analysis, visual
  quality control of assemblies, estimates of sequence divergence for
  all pairs of a set of genomes, and extraction of multiple alignments
  of selected regions across many species.
- It must run reproducibly on a Slurm cluster, in the cloud and on a
  workstation, and be maintained by a small team.

Write a design document covering: aligner and the alternatives
considered; how parameters are chosen; input preparation; processing of
the raw alignments; outputs and formats; quality control and reporting;
resource use and scaling; testing; failure modes with real-world
assemblies. Justify each choice and state what you are unsure of.
Design it yourself: do not search for or reuse an existing pipeline.
