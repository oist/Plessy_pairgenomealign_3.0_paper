Design Document: A Comparative Genome Assembly Alignment Pipeline

1. Goals and Scope

Objective

Align a set of query genome assemblies against a single target assembly across an extremely broad range of evolutionary distances and assembly qualities, producing outputs suitable for:

- Synteny analysis
- Structural rearrangement and breakpoint detection
- Assembly quality control
- Pairwise divergence estimation
- Extraction of multi-species alignments for selected loci
- Comparative genomics visualization

Constraints

The pipeline must:

- Support assemblies from Mb to tens of Gb.
- Handle chromosome-scale and highly fragmented assemblies.
- Work from nearly identical haplotypes to deeply diverged species.
- Run reproducibly on:
  - Slurm HPC
  - Cloud environments
  - Single workstations
- Follow nf-core design principles.
- Be maintainable by a small team.
- Avoid excessive tuning by end users.

Each query-target comparison is independent and naturally parallelizable.

The pipeline should not attempt whole-genome multiple alignment.

Instead it should produce high-quality pairwise alignments that can later be combined as needed.

3. Alignment Engine Choice

Primary Aligner

I would choose minimap2 as the primary alignment engine.

Why minimap2

It provides:

- Excellent scalability
- Mature assembly-to-assembly support
- Good performance across large genomes
- Robust handling of fragmented assemblies
- Active maintenance
- Predictable memory requirements

Importantly, minimap2 spans a wider range of divergence than most alternatives while remaining computationally practical at mammalian and plant genome scales.

For a small maintenance team, robustness matters more than marginal sensitivity gains.

Alternatives Considered

- Nucmer
- Wfmash
- LastZ
- MashMap
- Graph-based methods

Decision: retain minimap2 as primary engine, with potential optional backends in future.

Final Recommendation

1. Minimap2 as primary engine.
2. Divergence-aware parameter selection.
3. PAF as canonical internal representation.
4. Chaining, synteny blocks, and ambiguity tracking as first-class outputs.
5. Rich QC, especially dot plots.
6. Modular architecture allowing future alternative alignment backends.

Most importantly, alignment should be treated as a complete comparative genomics workflow rather than as a single alignment step.
